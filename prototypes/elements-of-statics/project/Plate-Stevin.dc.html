Plate · Simon Stevin, 1586 After Book I

The Wreath of Spheres.

A loop of equal beads hung over two slopes does not move. If it did, it would move for ever.

The loop cannot run by itself: a world where beads circle for ever would be a machine for nothing.

The hanging part is the same on both sides; take it away (Ax. III) and the rest still stands. Therefore
on the long slope pull as hard as on the short one.

Many beads on a gentle slope, few on a steep one: the pull along a slope grows with its steepness, just as Prop. IV found with wedges.

Stevin had it carved on his title page: wonder, and yet no wonder.
