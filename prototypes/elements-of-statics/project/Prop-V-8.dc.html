Book V · Cables and arches Prop. VIII

Proposition VIII. Theorem.

Tie an arch and a chain of the same shape to the same two ends, and join them with posts. The arch pushes out as hard as the chain pulls in, so the supports feel no thrust at all.

Both curves are the string polygon of equal loads (Book II, Prop. V), one standing, one hanging (Prop. III). The posts hand half of every load down to the chain, so each curve carries half.

Same shape, same share, so the same thrust: at each end pushes outward exactly as hard as pulls inward, and the two cancel inside the truss.

What is left for the supports is only the weight, straight down: an arch that sits on a roller.

Brunel's Royal Albert Bridge at Saltash (1859) is this proposition in wrought iron. Book IV meets Book V here.
