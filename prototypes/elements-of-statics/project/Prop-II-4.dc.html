Book II · Forces in the plane Prop. IV

Proposition IV. Theorem.

A load spread along a beam acts as one force: as large as the area of the load, and passing through its centre.

= through its middle; = two thirds along.

Each small arrow is a force (Ax. II adds them two by two), and the pile of them is the area. The black arrow turns the beam about any point exactly as the whole pile does (Prop. II), so it must stand at the centre.

Snow on a roof, water in a tank, people on a bridge: one arrow each, if you know where to put it.
