Book the fifth. Of cables and arches.

Proposition III.

Theorem.

As the chain hangs, so the arch stands, upside down. Every pull in the chain becomes a push in the arch, of the same size, and nothing else changes.

Each knot of the hanging chain holds three forces in balance, and so the chain is the string polygon of its weights. Turn the figure over and reverse every force: balance survives both, the loads point down again, and the becomes the . A chain can only pull, so it never bends; an arch of this shape is only ever pushed.

Q.E.D.
