Book V · Cables and arches Prop. III

Proposition III. Theorem.

As the chain hangs, so the arch stands, upside down. Every pull in the chain becomes a push in the arch, and nothing else changes.

The chain finds its own shape: every knot is three forces in balance (Book I, Prop. I), so the chain is the string polygon of its weights (Book II, Prop. V).

Turn the picture over, then reverse every force. Balance survives both, so the loads point down again, and becomes with the same size, the opposite sense.

A chain can only pull, so it never bends; an arch of this shape is only ever pushed, so its stones never need mortar.

Ut pendet continuum flexile, sic stabit contiguum rigidum inversum. Robert Hooke, 1675.
