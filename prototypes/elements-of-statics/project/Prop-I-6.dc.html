Proposition VI. Theorem.

A rope over a smooth pulley pulls equally on both sides; and the pulley hangs along the line that halves the angle between them.

= for a smooth wheel cannot hold the rope back on either side; equal lines, one colour.
+ + = 0 (Prop. I) and two equal sides make a rhombus, whose diagonal halves the angle.

The rod is not drawn crooked: it leans because the rope does.

Corollary. Pass the rope round more wheels, and a weight hung on n strands asks only one n-th of itself from your hand.
