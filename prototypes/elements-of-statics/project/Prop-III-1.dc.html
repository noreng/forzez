Book III · Bodies and supports Prop. I

Proposition I. Theorem.

A rough floor can answer any push that falls inside its cone. A push that falls outside it, the floor cannot answer, and the body slides.

A light block feels two forces: the push, and the answer of the floor. By Ax. I the answer must be equal, opposite, and on the same line as the push.

A smooth floor can only answer straight up. A rough one can lean, but no further than allows. Inside it, holds. Outside, is asked of a floor that has none to give.

Note that how hard you push does not matter, only where it points. Push twice as hard at the same angle, and the floor answers twice as hard.

Corollary. Tilt the floor until its slope matches the cone: a block laid on it is just about to slide. That angle is the whole of friction.
