Book II · Forces in the plane Prop. V

Proposition V. Problem.

To find the shape of a string carrying weights: stack the weights into a line, choose a pole, and draw rays. Every piece of the string runs parallel to a ray.

Each knot holds three forces: a weight and two pulls of the string. By Prop. I they close a triangle, and here every triangle shares the pole.
So in the string ∥ from the pole, and the length of the ray is the pull in that piece.

Move the pole and the string sags more or less; the weights are the same, only the pull changes.

Corollary. Turn the figure upside down, and the string becomes an arch that stands in pure compression. Gaudí hung his churches this way. (Book V.)
