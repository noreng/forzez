The Elements of Statics Book the fifth: cables and arches Proposition III

Proposition III.
Theorem.

As the chain hangs, so the arch stands, upside down. Every pull becomes a push of the same size.

Each knot of the chain holds three forces in balance, so the chain is the string polygon of its weights. Turn the figure over and reverse every force: balance survives both, and the becomes the . A chain never bends; an arch of this shape is only ever pushed. Q.E.D.

Ut pendet continuum flexile, sic stabit contiguum rigidum inversum.

Robert Hooke, 1675. As hangs the flexible line, so but inverted will stand the rigid arch.
