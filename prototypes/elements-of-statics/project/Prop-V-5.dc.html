Proposition V. Theorem.

A half circle, hinged at its feet and its crown and loaded at the crown, carries the load along two straight chords, not along the circle. Where chord and circle part, the arch must bend.

Each half is hinged at both ends and loaded at neither, so it is a two-force body: the force runs straight from hinge to hinge (Ax. I), along the dashed chord. By symmetry each chord takes half the load upward, so it carries the load divided by √2.

At the foot and at the crown the circle runs at 45° to the chord, and the push along the arch is exactly half the load. Halfway up, the circle runs parallel to and carries all of it, but stands far from it: the gap times the force is the bending.

The circle is the shape the eye likes. The chords are the shape the load likes. An arch that follows the load needs no bending at all (Prop. III).
