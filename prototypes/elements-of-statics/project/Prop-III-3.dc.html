Proposition III. Problem.

A roller can only answer square to its own slope. Tilt the roller, and the answer tilts with it; the pin at the other end must follow.

A roller cannot hold anything along its slope; it would roll. So its answer stands square to the slope, and its line is known before anything is solved.

The beam feels three forces: The first two lines meet above the beam, so the third must aim there too (Book I, Prop. I), and the triangle gives all three sizes.

Corollary. A level roller makes the pin answer straight up as well. Tilt it, and the pin begins to lean, though nobody touched the pin.
