Book IV · Trusses Prop. II

Proposition II. Theorem.

A square of pinned bars folds under the lightest push. Add one diagonal and it stands: a truss is made of triangles because only the triangle cannot change its shape.

Four bars and four pins leave the square one way to move: the corners stay pinned, the angles do not. No bar is stretched or squeezed by leaning over, so no bar resists it.

The diagonal cuts the square into two triangles, and three bars fix three sides, which fix the angles (Euclid I.8). Now the push must stretch or squeeze them, and they answer.

Note the two thin bars: in this square, under this push, they carry nothing. That is Proposition III.
