The bicycle wheel

Spokes that push by pulling less

A spoke can only pull. Pull every spoke tight first, and the few at the bottom can carry the rider by pulling less.

Tightening the spokes squeezes the rim and stretches every spoke : a wheel in balance with nothing on it. Put a rider on the hub. The ground pushes up at one point, and only the spokes above it notice: they lose part of their pull , and the rest hardly change. So the hub stands on its bottom spokes as if they were struts, though no spoke ever pushes. Q.E.D.

Corollary. Load it past its prestress and a bottom spoke goes slack. Prestress is borrowed compression, and it must be paid back. After Jobst Brandt, The Bicycle Wheel, 1981.
